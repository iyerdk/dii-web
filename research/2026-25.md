### Connectivity: FCC Second Submarine Cable Order — SLTE Security Closed Loop

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- June 4, 2026: FCC issues draft Second Report and Order on submarine cable landing licenses; vote scheduled June 25, 2026
- New framework extends US regulatory authority to Submarine Line Terminal Equipment (SLTE) — hardware converting optical signals at US cable-landing points into terrestrial network signals
- Blanket licence granted to all non-disqualified SLTE owners/operators; entities on Covered List or subject to foreign adversary designation must file annual SLTE Foreign Adversary Annual Report
- Chinese entities blocked from indirectly "operating" US-landing cables through SLTE ownership — closing the final end-to-end security gap in the existing landing licence regime
- Complements EU Cable Security Toolbox (released early June 2026): parallel US/EU governance frameworks now forming around the same physical infrastructure

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The FCC's June 25 vote on its Second Submarine Cable Report and Order closes the final security gap in US control over cable-landing infrastructure. Previously, a Chinese entity was barred from owning or operating a US-landing cable system but could still own the Submarine Line Terminal Equipment — the hardware at the cable head that converts optical signals into terrestrial data. The new order creates a blanket licence framework for non-disqualified SLTE operators while requiring entities on the Covered List (which includes Huawei, ZTE, and affiliates) to file annual reports. In practice, this bars HMN Technologies (formerly Huawei Marine Networks) from end-terminal equipment on US-landing cables — a direct parallel to Germany's exclusion of Huawei/ZTE from 5G core networks. For European cable operators, the US compliance layer is new: any consortium cable landing in the US must now audit its SLTE vendor against the Covered List. With the EU's Cable Security Toolbox released last week and GUIDE's 17-nation military information-sharing framework launched May 30, three parallel governance architectures are now forming around the same physical submarine cable plant. The US SLTE order is the most technically specific intervention to date.

**What to watch:** June 25 FCC vote outcome and any dissent; European cable consortia needing SLTE vendor transitions; whether the EU Cable Security Toolbox follow-through adopts an equivalent SLTE-level standard; GUIDE operational protocols for SLTE-related intelligence sharing.

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
Romania's June 30 legal merger completes the second four-to-three mobile consolidation in the EU within three months, following the UK's VodafoneThree closure in April. The deal structure merits close attention for what it reveals about acceptable remedy architecture: OTE — the Deutsche Telekom subsidiary and Telekom Romania parent — sold the postpaid customer book and technical infrastructure to Vodafone for €30M while Digi separately acquired the prepaid base, spectrum, and towers for €40M. Total: €70M for a national operator, implying a near-zero enterprise value for a network that would cost hundreds of millions to rebuild. The key structural point is Digi's spectrum acquisition — adding licensed bands at €40M that would command €200M+ in a competitive spectrum auction. Digi's strengthened spectrum position makes it a more credible capacity rival to Orange and the enlarged Vodafone, providing regulators with the argument that the three-player market retains a well-capitalised challenger. This is the template the Autorité de la concurrence will be watching: if AdC demands structural remedies in the SFR process, redirecting spectrum or assets to a challenger operator (rather than blocking the consolidation outright) is what Romania — and the UK — demonstrate is politically and legally achievable. The consumer price impact in Romania over 12–18 months will be the empirical test for the SFR consolidation thesis.

**What to watch:** Romania post-merger consumer price data (the empirical test for SFR/EU consolidation economics); Digi Romania spectrum deployment and network quality trajectory; OTE repatriation of Romania exit proceeds to Greek capex; whether AdC cites Romania in any SFR pre-notification feedback.
