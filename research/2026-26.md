The Marseille landing is structurally notable: Orange connects ViaTunisia directly into its data centre fibre ring, meaning the new capacity is immediately available to Orange's wholesale and enterprise customers without an additional transit hop. For investors tracking the submarine cable sovereignty theme, this development amplifies France's position relative to Italy's TIM Sparkle play — Rome controls Mediterranean subsea routing contracts via the MEF/Retelit acquisition, but Marseille now holds Europe's densest cable hub with 17 landings. ### Energy & Power: Germany PUE 1.2 Triggers July 1 — EU-Wide Data Centre Labelling Regulation Finalised

- https://emea.socomec.com/en/news/eu-energy-efficiency-directive-what-new-rules-mean-data-centres

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- ~June 10, 2026: European Commission finalises the second EED delegated regulation under Directive 2023/1791 — establishes an EU-wide data centre rating and labelling scheme across 24 Key Performance Indicators (PUE, WUE, ERF, REF and others)
- July 1, 2026 (9 days): Germany's Energy Efficiency Act triggers hard performance requirements for all new data centres commissioned from that date: PUE ≤1.2 (vs typical European new-build of 1.4–1.6), minimum 15% waste heat reuse
- Germany January 2027 follow-on: 100% renewable electricity supply mandatory for all German data centres
- EU-wide existing obligation (in force from June 2024): any DC with ≥500kW installed IT power demand must report annually to the European Database on Data Centres; the June 2026 labelling regulation adds a public performance score layer on top
- Compliance cost implication: achieving PUE ≤1.2 in new German builds requires liquid cooling infrastructure investment — estimated €5–15M premium on a 50MW facility relative to an air-cooled baseline

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
Germany is the first EU member state to legislate hard performance thresholds for data centres, and the July 1, 2026 trigger arrives in nine days. The PUE ≤1.2 requirement for any facility commissioned from that date is more demanding than the typical European new-build benchmark of 1.4–1.6, and it coincides with the EC's finalisation of the EU-wide labelling regulation, which will publish performance scores publicly via the European Database on Data Centres. For operators with projects in the German pipeline, the compliance calculus is now clear: liquid cooling design is no longer optional — it is a regulatory requirement for German projects, and a differentiating factor across FLAP-D markets where Germany has historically competed on stability and proximity to Central European networks. The €5–15M liquid cooling premium on a 50MW build at scale is manageable within European greenfield capex envelopes (~€400–800M for a hyperscale campus), but it establishes a German-specific cost floor that other markets, including France and the Netherlands, are watching before legislating equivalents. The EU-wide labelling scheme creates public transparency pressure: operators whose EU sites score poorly on WUE (water), ERF (energy reuse), or REF (renewables) will face investor and enterprise-customer scrutiny ahead of a labelling framework that France, Netherlands, Ireland, and Belgium have signalled they may adopt more aggressively post-Germany.

**What to watch:** First German new-build campus commencing construction post-July 1 — whether liquid cooling is standard; France and Netherlands response (both monitor Germany's threshold as a reference point for their own national frameworks); EU labelling scheme publication and public database launch date; whether the European Commission moves from labelling to mandatory performance thresholds EU-wide in the EED review cycle.

### European Telecom: EC Revised Merger Guidelines Consultation Closes June 26 — SFR Stakes Rise
Thread: european-telecom-consolidation
Sources:

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- June 26, 2026 (4 days): Public consultation closes on the European Commission's Draft Revised Horizontal Merger Guidelines — the first rewrite of the 2004 merger framework in 22 years
- Draft guidelines: abandon the 2-year investment benefit horizon used to justify consolidation; codify "loss of investment competition" as a standalone theory of harm — applicable to 4→3 consolidations where an operator was previously a competitive investment driver
- Telecom industry response: GSMA and ETNO submitted feedback this week arguing the draft framework does not go far enough in recognising the investment rationale for consolidation — specifically the underinvestment problem in fragmented four-player markets
- SFR direct relevance: any Phase 1 or Phase 2 review of the SFR three-way acquisition will be conducted under the final guidelines (adoption expected late 2026, before formal SFR notification H2 2026)
- AdC monitoring: French competition authority president Coeuré (Edition 16: "ne va pas de soi") has cited the 2005 French price-collusion case; the final EC guidelines will define the investment-harm framework AdC must engage with in parallel domestic review

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The June 26 consultation deadline marks the end of the public comment phase on the most consequential competition policy revision for European telecom in a generation. The draft guidelines' abandonment of the 2-year investment benefit horizon is a double-edged sword for SFR: operators can no longer rely on a simple forward-looking investment case to pass a Phase 1 screen, but the codification of "loss of investment competition" as a standalone harm also gives regulators a new tool to challenge deals where a formerly aggressive capital-investing operator is absorbed. For SFR specifically, the AdC's exposure is defined by this framework: Bouygues has been the most aggressive French mobile network investor of the past five years; its integration into the consolidated entity means the French market loses Bouygues as an independent investment-competing operator, precisely the harm theory the draft guidelines articulate. Telecom industry lobbying via GSMA and ETNO — submitted this week — argues for an investment productivity test that weighs scale benefits; if the Commission accepts modifications, it would shift the framework back toward permissiveness. Final guidelines adoption in Q4 2026 runs concurrently with SFR's expected formal pre-notification window, creating a race between regulatory framework finalisation and deal filing that both sides will be tracking closely.

**What to watch:** Final guidelines publication (Q4 2026) and whether the investment competition theory of harm is moderated in response to industry consultation; SFR formal pre-notification timing relative to guidelines adoption; whether AdC cites the new framework in any public statement ahead of formal filing; EC's treatment of the Romanian 4→3 precedent (very low remedies) vs the SFR transaction scale.
