**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The EC's unconditional approval of the TIM Sparkle acquisition represents the Italian state's most significant infrastructure intervention since the NetCo fibre separation. MEF acquiring direct control of Italy's international submarine cable platform — routing European traffic via the Mediterranean to Gulf, African, and Asian markets — is explicitly strategic: Italy controls its own cable landing and routing infrastructure at a moment when Hormuz and Red Sea disruption (subsea-cable-disruption thread) has elevated the value of alternative Mediterranean transit. The Retelit co-buyer is also notable: Cellnex-owned Retelit brings operational expertise in Italian wholesale networking while introducing a Spanish private operator alongside the Italian state — a hybrid structure that passed EC scrutiny without conditions. For the broader European connectivity picture, TIM Sparkle's nationalisation adds to a clear pattern: France intervening in Altice SFR, Germany mandating Huawei removal, Italy directly acquiring its international cable gateway. EC competition law has not blocked any of these state-intervention moves, setting a permissive precedent. - European DC market is actively decentralising beyond FLAP-D (Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris, Dublin): this week alone HSCALE closed 250MW in Milan (€2B), Nscale confirmed 520MW potential at Narvik (Norway hydro), and Digital Realty launched BCN1 Barcelona — all power-first secondary market selections

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
EDCA's €176B forecast and power-constraint warning is the clearest industry-level articulation of a structural shift DII has been tracking across individual investments: capital is abundant for European digital infrastructure; power is the scarce input. The implication is a geographic resorting of investment — away from legacy FLAP-D markets where grid capacity is exhausted and power costs are high, and toward power-surplus regions with credible renewable supply at competitive cost. This week's project cohort is the evidence: HSCALE chose Northwest Milan (Alpine hydro access), Nscale chose Narvik (Norwegian hydro, Eksfin-backed), Digital Realty chose Barcelona (Spanish solar), each a deliberate power-first site selection. For infrastructure investors, this creates a two-segment market: legacy FLAP-D assets trade at a premium for contracted capacity that cannot be replicated, while new-build premium flows to power-surplus secondary markets. The EU-level policy implication is that electricity market reform, grid expansion permitting, and interconnector investment are now the gating variables for European digital infrastructure competitiveness — a framing energy ministers have not yet internalised. Watch: national grid expansion plans from Germany, Ireland, and Poland in H2 2026; Ofgem's UK grid connection queue resolution; and whether any EU member state links digital infrastructure strategy to power market design.
