**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- EU Digital Networks Act adopted by European Commission on January 21, 2026; now in European Parliament–Council trialogue
- DNA proposes indefinite spectrum licences by default — or 40-year maximum with quasi-automatic renewal, up from the current 20-year cap
- Merges four legal instruments: EECC, BEREC Regulation, Radio Spectrum Policy Programme, and Open Internet Regulation
- European Commission estimates: full DNA deployment could add up to €400B to EU GDP by 2035
- GSMA study (concurrent): Europe needs €475B to complete its 5G journey; only ~€270B is accessible, leaving a €205B investment gap

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The EU Digital Networks Act is the regulatory complement to the consolidation wave tracked in the european-telecom-consolidation thread. Where the EU Horizontal Merger Guidelines (Q4 2026 adoption) address market structure, the DNA addresses the underlying investment economics. Indefinite spectrum is its most consequential provision: European mobile operators currently face a "spectrum cliff" every 20 years — a renewal cost event that weighs on balance sheets and depresses long-duration capex. Eliminating that cliff could be worth €5–15B in risk-adjusted balance sheet value per large operator. Combined with the "use-it-or-share-it-or-lose-it" safeguards that prevent spectrum hoarding, indefinite licences should stimulate both network rollout and secondary spectrum trading. The GSMA's €205B investment gap framing is the industry's benchmark response — arguing that regulatory concessions on spectrum are necessary before operators can credibly commit to 5G Standalone and 6G build programmes. The trialogue will negotiate how much authority sits at the EU versus national level, and whether net neutrality rules are preserved unchanged. Completion expected by end-2026 — concurrent with SFR pre-notification and EU Merger Guidelines finalisation. A landmark quarter for European telecom policy.

### Energy & Power: Germany's dual July 1 mandate — PUE ≤1.2 and ERF ≥10% — is now live

Thread: eu-dc-efficiency-standards
Sources:
- https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/data-center-requirements-under-new-german-energy-efficiency-act
- https://www.moduledge.com/blog/eu-data-center-regulations-2026
- https://www.cundall.com/ideas/blog/has-the-german-energy-efficiency-act-become-a-thorn-in-the-side-of-german-data-centre-operators

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- From July 1, 2026: all new German data centres must operate at PUE ≤1.2 AND Energy Reuse Factor (ERF) ≥10% — no grandfather clause
- ERF escalation confirmed: ≥15% (July 2027), ≥20% (July 2028); existing DCs face PUE ≤1.5 from July 2027, PUE ≤1.3 from July 2030
- ERF paradox: AI GPU liquid cooling operates at 35–45°C; German district heating networks require 70–90°C injection temperature — heat pump/exchanger hardware required for compliance
- Capital cost of ERF compliance on a 50MW AI-dense build: €5–15M beyond standard liquid cooling premium
- Annual reporting to BAFA via Germany's energy efficiency register (RZReg) required; non-compliance penalties reach €50K–€100K per infraction per Jan 2027 renewables mandate (100% renewable electricity for all German DCs)

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
July 1 marks the operative date for Europe's first hard dual data centre efficiency threshold. The PUE requirement is technically achievable — liquid cooling already meets ≤1.2 for high-density AI builds. The ERF requirement is the structural challenge. AI data centres generate waste heat at 35–45°C because GPU liquid cooling runs at those temperatures; German district heating networks require 70–90°C for injection. The gap requires heat pump infrastructure that adds €5–15M to a 50MW build — on top of the liquid cooling premium itself. Operators have three practical responses: (1) install heat pump stacks (cost and complexity), (2) co-locate low-density loads with compatible waste heat profiles (limits AI density), or (3) build outside Germany. Option 3 benefits Norway and Sweden directly — Nordic district heating networks typically operate at 70–80°C and can accept GPU waste heat with lower heat pump uplift, and ambient temperatures reduce cooling energy requirements. France, the Netherlands, Ireland, and Belgium are monitoring Germany's first post-July 1 planning applications as their own legislative benchmark. The first approved German AI campus post-July 1 will provide the definitive compliance cost data point.

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- I-2SEA (India–Southeast Asia) construction contracts signed July 4, 2026 by consortium: Lightstorm (lead), Microsoft, Singtel, and Tata Communications
- Route: India to Singapore and Kuala Lumpur; entirely bypasses Red Sea, Hormuz, and Suez chokepoints
- Technical specification explicitly designed for AI training and GPU cluster traffic — low jitter, high capacity for large data packet workloads
- No European landing point — purely an India–SEA system; complements rather than replaces European-facing cable capacity
- Construction start follows FCC adoption of SLTE Second R&O (June 25, 2026) barring Huawei/ZTE/HMN from landing equipment at US cable stations

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
I-2SEA is structurally significant for two reasons beyond its geography. First, it is the first publicly documented cable system explicitly specified for AI training traffic — low jitter and high capacity for GPU cluster inter-node communication, rather than standard internet/CDN payloads. This signals that subsea cable design is bifurcating: AI hyperscale operators are building dedicated fabric for training runs that requires different engineering trade-offs (latency consistency over burst throughput) than traditional internet cables. Second, Microsoft's participation alongside Singtel and Tata Communications follows the pattern established by Google's Grace Hopper (US-Spain-UK), Meta's 2Africa, and Amazon's multiple EMEA cables — hyperscalers are building proprietary cable capacity in corridors that avoid geopolitically exposed chokepoints. I-2SEA directly bypasses the Hormuz/Red Sea corridor that has disrupted 17 cables since 2026-Q1 (subsea-cable-disruption thread). For European infrastructure operators, the implication is architectural: as AI-native cable systems build out the India-SEA corridor independently, traffic that previously transited the Mediterranean-Suez-Indian Ocean route increasingly routes directly, reducing (but not eliminating) the strategic importance of the disrupted Red Sea corridor. Forward watch: whether Lightstorm or a consortium member announces a complementary European leg connecting I-2SEA's India landing points to EMEA.
