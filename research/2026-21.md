**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- The UK government has secured parliamentary backing for regulations allowing major data centre developments to opt into the Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project (NSIP) regime — the same planning track used for power stations, motorways, and airports
- NSIP opt-in aims to streamline planning approvals for large data centres and explicitly align data centre consent with energy infrastructure delivery
- In parallel, Ofcom CEO Dame Melanie Dawes confirmed data centres will be brought within Ofcom's cybersecurity oversight remit via the forthcoming Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, extending existing telecoms duties to the sector
- Ofcom is expected to publish its final decision on a new fixed data connectivity services regulatory framework in March 2026, effective April 2026 to March 2031
- UK context: industrial electricity at 24.67p/kWh (~4x US rates) and the OpenAI Stargate UK pause (Edition 6) make planning speed the lever government can actually pull without touching energy pricing

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The UK government's decision to extend NSIP eligibility to large data centres is a direct policy response to the planning bottleneck that has hampered UK data centre growth relative to Ireland and the Netherlands. NSIP status moves approvals to a nationally determined process, bypassing local planning authorities and reducing the timeline from years to months for qualifying projects. The explicit linking of data centre consent to energy infrastructure delivery signals that the government wants to avoid stranded-planning scenarios where a data centre gets permission but grid connection is years away — the constraint that derailed several hyperscaler UK plans in 2025. The simultaneous Ofcom cybersecurity extension creates a new compliance layer for data centre operators: facilities will need to meet telecoms-grade security standards under Ofcom supervision, adding governance overhead but also providing regulatory certainty that large enterprise and public-sector customers increasingly require. For infrastructure investors, the planning regime change is the more material near-term development: it lowers the execution risk premium on UK data centre assets, which has been a persistent discount factor relative to Nordic or Dutch sites. Watch for: first NSIP applications from hyperscalers or co-lo operators, and Ofcom's consultation timeline for the new data centre security framework.
