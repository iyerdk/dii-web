### UK & Ireland: Ireland CRU mandates data centres generate and export power — 80% renewables within 6 years

Thread: new

- https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/cru-publishes-long-awaited-final-policy-on-data-centre-connections/

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- Ireland's Commission for Regulation of Utilities (CRU) has issued its final Large Energy User connection policy decision; applies to all data centres with Maximum Import Capacity (MIC) ≥ 1 MVA
- Operators must source ≥80% of annual electricity from additional renewables generated within the Republic of Ireland, achieved via a "glide-up" within six years of grid energisation
- Each new connection must build and operate generation or storage capacity equal to its MIC — and that capacity must participate in the wholesale electricity market (not just serve the facility)
- System operators required to publish data centre connection engagement process by March 31, 2026; CRU may review the 1 MVA de-minimis threshold in future
- Ireland hosts approximately 800 MW of data centre load on the Dublin-area grid; Eirgrid has previously flagged that DCs accounted for ~21% of national electricity demand

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The CRU's decision fundamentally reprices the cost of new data centre capacity in Ireland. Previously, operators secured grid connections as standard large electricity customers. The new framework converts them into quasi-generators: any facility drawing ≥1 MVA must build matching generation or storage, sell excess into the wholesale market, and hit 80% Irish-sourced renewables within six years. This is not a soft ESG target — it is a grid condition of connection, with CRU holding enforcement authority. The policy reflects the political reality that Ireland's 800 MW+ of data centre load has materially constrained grid headroom in the Greater Dublin area, and that Eirgrid's system adequacy obligations have been strained as a result. For operators, the capital model changes: every site now bundles data centre build with energy infrastructure development. For infrastructure investors, Ireland's premium DC market (low-latency access to transatlantic cable landing stations, EU data residency, English-language jurisdiction) now comes with embedded energy capex — implying higher entry costs but also potential wholesale market revenue. In the context of the UK Stargate pause (thread: `uk-ai-energy-gap`), Ireland's rules create a harder cost environment than previously; operators evaluating Dublin vs. Manchester vs. the Nordics will now model energy capex explicitly. **Watch:** whether EirGrid publishes the connection process by the March 31 deadline and how pipeline projects at scale (>100 MW) are handled.

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**

Saudi stc's $800M SilkLink investment is the first major private-sector response in the affected region. **Watch:** whether the US bill reaches full Senate floor vote, and whether the EU €20M triggers mandatory resilience requirements for European cable landing stations.
