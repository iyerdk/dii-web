### Western Europe: EU Commission publishes landmark merger guidelines overhaul — enables the European telecom consolidation wave
Thread: european-telecom-consolidation, dt-tmobile-merger
Sources:

- https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/May/EU-Merger-Control-New-Draft-Merger-Guidelines
- https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/european-commissions-new-draft-merger-guidelines-snapshot-overview-what-dealmakers

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- European Commission published Draft Revised Merger Guidelines April 30, 2026 — first consolidation of EU merger control guidance in over two decades (prior horizontal guidelines 2004, non-horizontal guidelines 2008)
- Public consultation open until June 26, 2026; final adoption expected Q4 2026 or early 2027
- Key telecom-favorable provision: abandons the rigid 2-year horizon for assessing merger investment benefits — directly relevant to capital-intensive infrastructure sectors with long capex cycles (telecom, energy, digital infrastructure)
- New provision: "loss of investment competition" codified as a standalone theory of harm — creates a new analytical category the Commission can deploy against mergers reducing network investment pressure between competitors
- Structural change: single comprehensive framework replaces two separate guidance documents; significantly more detailed, reducing regulatory uncertainty for complex multi-market deals such as the DT/T-Mobile US combination

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
The April 30 Draft Merger Guidelines publication is the most significant EU competition policy development for the European telecom consolidation wave, and was not reflected in DII coverage through Edition 13. The abandonment of the rigid 2-year benefit assessment window is structurally favorable for every major telecom M&A transaction currently active: SFR (France 4→3), Deutsche Telekom's reported T-Mobile US combination (dt-tmobile-merger thread), and any future Nordic or Southern European consolidation. Under the old framework, operators had to demonstrate that merger investment benefits would materialise within 2 years of closing — an impossible standard for infrastructure capex cycles running 5–10 years. The new framework allows proportionate evidence of medium-term investment benefits, materially lowering the analytical burden of proof. The "loss of investment competition" codification cuts the other way, creating a lever the Commission can apply against mergers where competitive bidding for network investment itself had market value. For the DT/T-Mobile US combination — which requires simultaneous FCC, DOJ, and EC review — the extended investment horizon is a meaningful improvement in the EU leg. For infrastructure investors, the practical effect is a clearer, more predictable merger analysis framework from Q4 2026 onwards. Watch: stakeholder responses to the June 26 consultation, particularly from incumbent telcos and national competition authorities; and whether the Commission addresses 4→3 mobile market consolidation explicitly in the final text.

**Key facts (5 bullets):**
- European data centre PPA volumes fell 38%: 4.2 GW (2024) → 2.6 GW (2025) — source: Rystad Energy
- Offshore wind PPA segment — previously the largest single category — collapsed: 1.35 GW (2024) → 0.5 GW (2025) → just 100 MW signed in all of Q1 2026
- Wider European PPA market across all sectors: deal count down 60% year-on-year; contracted capacity down 40% vs 2024
- Three structural causes: (1) offshore wind project delivery delays remove large-volume contracts; (2) pricing disconnect — falling capture rates and rising negative wholesale electricity price hours erode developer economics, forcing above-market PPA ask prices; (3) data centre operators pivoting toward nuclear and hybrid baseload structures for always-on supply
- Consequence: European hyperscaler and co-location operators face a structural gap between buildout pace (EDCA €176B forecast, Edition 13) and available contracted clean power supply

**Research narrative (150–200 words):**
Rystad Energy's analysis reveals a structural fracture in the European clean energy procurement model for data centres: capacity plans are accelerating while the mechanism supposed to power them is in retreat. The 100 MW offshore wind PPA figure for Q1 2026 is stark — offshore wind was projected as Europe's primary DC clean power vehicle through the mid-2030s. The structural causes are supply-side, not demand-side: wind project delays remove available contracts, and negative wholesale electricity hours erode solar capture rates, making PPAs structurally unattractive to developers at prices offtakers will accept. The result is a pricing standoff between developer economics and operator budgets. Nuclear and hybrid structures are filling the gap in the US (Amazon/Talen, Google/Elementl 1.8 GW, Microsoft/Three Mile Island), while European supply of nuclear PPA capacity remains constrained by fleet age, regulation, and geography. In Europe, the beneficiaries of the offshore wind PPA drought are power-surplus markets with baseload alternatives: Norwegian hydro (Narvik, Edition 13), Swedish hydro and nuclear, and Iberian solar-plus-storage. The PPA market retreat simultaneously reinforces the nordics-infra-advantage thread and sharpens the energy constraint in legacy FLAP-D markets. For infrastructure investors, a DC asset without long-term power procurement certainty is structurally mispriced; this data suggests that certainty is getting harder to secure across most of Western Europe. Watch: European utility Q2 2026 earnings for PPA pipeline confirmation; UK Contracts for Difference (CfD) auction outcomes as a proxy for offshore wind economics; and whether any EU member state launches a preferential clean-power DC tariff regime.
